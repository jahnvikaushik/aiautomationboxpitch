AI Automation Box: sales cheat sheet

How to spot an AI opportunity, what to ask, what to listen for, and why this customer would buy the box.

The 20-second pitch

ChatGPT plus a digital assistant, in a box in your own office. Everyone on your team gets unlimited AI, your data never leaves your network, and you pay once instead of per person, every month.

Three things to sniff out in any customer

  • Repetition: the same questions, reports or chasing, done by hand.
  • Documents: lots of files, SOPs or contracts that people read, search or summarise.
  • Sensitivity: data they would not paste into a public AI tool.

Any one is a conversation. Two or more is a real opportunity.

Open with these, in any industry

  • "Is anyone on your team using ChatGPT or similar today?"
  • "What are they allowed to put into it? What are they not allowed to?"
  • "What task does your team repeat every week that nobody enjoys?"

Easy objections

  • "We already have ChatGPT / Copilot." Ask what they can't put in it. The box handles that data, and cost doesn't grow per seat.
  • "Is it as good as the big names?" It runs strong open models, and better ones can be loaded free. Prove it on their real work in the 4 to 8 week PoC rather than arguing in a meeting.
  • "Our IT is small." MyRepublic installs and hands over. Staff build agents with no code, and IT controls roles.

Probably not a fit yet

  • No repetitive or document-heavy work, and no data concerns.
  • Wants a closed frontier model specifically, with no appetite to test open models.
  • Many staff chatting at once: a Basic box serves about 30 to 80 for interactive chat. Go Pro (S$30,988) or Expert.
  • Needs it very fast at large scale: Expert builds take 20+ weeks. Basic and Pro ship from stock.

Next step to propose

Book a short use-case workshop, then a PoC of 4 to 8 weeks running 2 to 3 live automations in their own environment. Ask for their environment specs and top use cases. Pricing and sizing are indicative until MyRepublic's AI team confirms on consultation.
